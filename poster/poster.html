Economic growth and human well-being

Does a country's income tell us how well its people live? 148 countries, 2000–2022. Luyu Su & Yifan Zuo · STATS 401 · yyiffann.github.io/stats401-final-project

Why and questions

GDP per person is the default yardstick of progress, but says little about health, schooling, services, work and clean air.

Key measure: the well-being gap = well-being score minus the score expected at that income.

  • Where is prosperity, and where does well-being beat it?
  • How did countries' paths differ?
  • Who departs from the income pattern?
  • Which dimensions explain it?
  • Who moved to a higher stage?

Data and method

  • World Bank WDI + UNDP HDR + World Happiness Report + Natural Earth
  • 148 countries × 23 years = 3,404 rows; 10 indicators → 5 dimensions → 1 score (fixed goalposts)
  • Yearly LOWESS of score on log GDP gives the gap
  • 2,000 random weightings test robustness
  • k-means (k=5) development profiles at 4 snapshot years
  • Validation: score vs. life satisfaction r = 0.78
  • D3.js v7, one shared state links all views
